Section 179 Vehicle & Heavy Truck Deduction Calculator

Under Section 179 of the Internal Revenue Code (IRC), business owners can write off up to 100% of the purchase price of qualifying work vehicles, heavy trucks, and commercial equipment acquired and placed into service during the tax year, rather than depreciating them over multiple years.

This browser-based us-section-179-truck-calculator allows business owners, contractors, and fleet managers to evaluate statutory IRS tax deductions, heavy SUV $32,000 caps, 100% bonus depreciation rates, and net cash tax savings instantly and privately without transmitting financial records.

Important Statutory Qualification Guidelines

  • Macro Section 179 Limit ($2,560,000): The overall Section 179 expensing cap allows businesses to write off up to $2,560,000 of qualifying purchases, with a dollar-for-dollar phase-out threshold starting at $4,090,000 in total equipment acquisitions.
  • Heavy SUV Cap ($32,000): Vehicles categorized as passenger SUVs between 6,001 and 14,000 lbs GVWR face a specific Section 179 deduction limit of $32,000. However, any cost basis exceeding $32,000 is eligible for 100% Bonus Depreciation.
  • More Than 50% Business Use Requirement: To qualify for Section 179 or Bonus Depreciation, the vehicle must be used more than 50% for qualified business purposes. If business use drops to 50% or lower, both Section 179 and Bonus Depreciation drop to $0.00, forcing a fallback to pro-rated straight-line MACRS depreciation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What vehicles qualify for the 100% Section 179 heavy truck tax deduction?
Vehicles with a Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR) exceeding 6,000 lbs that are used more than 50% for business qualify. Heavy work trucks, cargo vans, specialty commercial equipment, and vehicles with no passenger seating behind the driver qualify for full expensing up to the $2,560,000 Section 179 limit.
What is the Section 179 deduction cap for heavy SUVs weighing between 6,001 and 14,000 lbs?
Passenger SUVs and crossover vehicles with a GVWR between 6,001 and 14,000 lbs are subject to a specific statutory Section 179 cap of $32,000. Remaining basis above this $32,000 cap is eligible for 100% Bonus Depreciation.
What happens if business use falls to 50% or below?
Under statutory IRS rules, if business usage is 50% or less, both Section 179 and Bonus Depreciation are strictly reduced to $0.00. The asset must then be depreciated over its standard recovery period using standard straight-line/MACRS depreciation models.
How does the annual Section 179 overall limit and phase-out threshold work?
The maximum Section 179 expensing limit is $2,560,000, with a dollar-for-dollar phase-out threshold starting at $4,090,000 in total equipment purchases. The deduction is fully phased out when total equipment acquisitions reach $6,650,000.
