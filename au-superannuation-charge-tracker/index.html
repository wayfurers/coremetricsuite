AU Superannuation Charge Tracker & SG Compliance Calculator

Under the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992, Australian employers are legally mandated to contribute a minimum statutory percentage of Ordinary Times Earnings (OTE) into eligible employee superannuation funds.

The au-superannuation-charge-tracker tool allows payroll managers, accountants, and business owners to track unpaid super contributions, assess statutory 10% per annum nominal interest charges, compute ATO administrative fees, and estimate total non-deductible Superannuation Guarantee Charge (SGC) liabilities.

Superannuation Guarantee Statutory Parameters & Penalties

Failing to pay employee superannuation contributions in full by the statutory quarterly due dates triggers an obligation to lodge an SGC Statement with the Australian Taxation Office (ATO).

Compliance Parameter Statutory Rate / Rule ATO Regulation Component
Statutory SG Rate 12.0% of OTE Mandatory minimum contribution rate for employee earnings.
SGC Nominal Interest 10.0% p.a. Accrues daily from the first day of the relevant quarter until SGC statement lodgment.
Administrative Component $20 per employee / quarter Fixed administrative charge levied on all late quarterly returns.
Tax Deductibility Status Strictly Non-Deductible SGC liabilities cannot be claimed as tax deductions, unlike on-time super.

Key Rules for Superannuation Guarantee Charge Compliance

  • Quarterly Due Dates: SG payments must clear into employee funds by the 28th of the month following quarter-end (Oct 28, Jan 28, Apr 28, Jul 28).
  • Salary & Wages Base vs OTE: On-time super is calculated on Ordinary Times Earnings (OTE). However, once super becomes late, SGC is calculated on total Salary and Wages (including overtime).
  • Nominal Interest Accrual: ATO nominal interest (10% p.a.) accrues from the beginning of the quarter in which the contribution was due, not from the late payment date.
  • Part 7 Penalties: Late lodgment of an SGC Statement can attract additional ATO Part 7 penalties up to 200% of the underlying SGC amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current Superannuation Guarantee (SG) rate in Australia?
The statutory Superannuation Guarantee (SG) rate is 12.0% of an employee's Ordinary Times Earnings (OTE). Employers must pay super contributions at least quarterly by the ATO statutory due dates or risk incurring the Superannuation Guarantee Charge (SGC).
How is the Superannuation Guarantee Charge (SGC) calculated on late payments?
When super is paid late or underpaid, the SGC is calculated on total salary and wages (not just OTE). It consists of three parts: the SG Shortfall amount, Nominal Interest (10% per annum calculated from the start of the quarter), and an Administration Fee of $20 per employee per quarter.
Are late Superannuation Guarantee Charge payments tax-deductible?
No. While regular, on-time superannuation contributions are tax-deductible business expenses, SGC payments made to the ATO (including the shortfall, nominal interest, and admin components) are strictly non-deductible under Australian tax law.
When is the Superannuation Guarantee payment due each quarter?
Quarterly SG payments must clear into the employee's super fund by the 28th day following the end of each quarter: Q1 (28 Oct), Q2 (28 Jan), Q3 (28 Apr), and Q4 (28 Jul).
