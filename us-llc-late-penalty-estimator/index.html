Key Rules for Pass-Through & Foreign-Owned Entities

Understanding how IRS penalties accrue is critical to mitigating financial risk:

  • Per-Partner / Shareholder Rule ($255/mo): Under IRC §6698 and §6699, pass-through entities (Form 1065 and Form 1120-S) face penalties multiplied by every owner involved, compounding monthly up to 12 full calendar months.
  • Form 5472 Initial & Continuation Fines: Foreign single-member LLCs face an automatic initial $25,000 failure-to-file fine. If the return remains unfiled 90 days after IRS notice issuance, additional continuation fines of $25,000 accrue for every 30-day period with no ceiling.
  • Zero Income Filings: Inactive entities or LLCs with $0 net revenue are not exempt from Form 5472 or Form 1065 penalties if informational filing obligations exist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the late filing penalty calculated for a multi-member US LLC (Form 1065)?
Under updated tax rules (IRC §6698), the IRS assesses a late filing penalty for Form 1065 at $255 per partner, per month (or fraction of a month) that the return is late, for a maximum duration of up to 12 months.
What is the penalty for late filing Form 5472 on a foreign-owned single-member LLC?
Under IRC §6038A, failing to timely file Form 5472 triggers a flat initial statutory penalty of $25,000 per year. If non-compliance continues beyond 90 days after IRS notification, additional continuation penalties of $25,000 for every 30-day period apply with no statutory upper limit.
What is the late penalty rate for S-Corporations filing Form 1120-S late?
Under IRC §6699, the IRS assesses a late filing penalty for Form 1120-S at the rate of $255 per shareholder, per month (or part of a month), capped at a maximum of 12 months.
Does an inactive or zero-revenue US LLC face late tax filing penalties?
Yes. Foreign-owned single-member LLCs must file Form 5472 even with zero gross revenue if there were any reportable transactions (such as initial capital contributions, owner-paid filing fees, or distributions). Failure to file results in the full $25,000 penalty regardless of income.
