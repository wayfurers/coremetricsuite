AU SCHADS Award Vehicle Allowance & Paid Travel Calculator

Under the Social, Community, Home Care and Disability Services Industry Award 2020 (SCHADS Award - MA000100), disability support workers, home care aides, and community service employees are entitled to financial compensation when using their personal vehicles for work obligations[cite: 1].

This browser-based au-schads-vehicle-allowance calculator assists employees, employers, and NDIS service providers in determining accurate motor vehicle allowances (Clause 20.5) and paid travel time allowances (Clause 25.5) for multi-client rosters, split shifts, and participant transport itineraries[cite: 1].

Key Rules for SCHADS Vehicle & Travel Reimbursements

  • Vehicle Kilometre Allowance: If you use your personal car to drive between clients or transport a client during a shift, your employer must pay $1.05 per km driven[cite: 1].
  • Travel Time is Paid Work Time: Clause 25.5 explicitly establishes that the time spent traveling between consecutive appointments during a shift must be treated as paid working time at your applicable hourly rate[cite: 1].
  • Commuting Boundaries: Your standard morning commute from home to your first client, and your evening commute from your final client back home, is generally unpaid and non-reimbursable unless special remote locality provisions apply[cite: 1].
  • NDIS Provider Billing vs Award Payment: While NDIS billing rules determine what an agency can charge a participant, employer obligations under the SCHADS Award are legally binding regarding what must be paid to the employee[cite: 1].

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the SCHADS Award motor vehicle allowance rate per kilometre?
Under Clause 20.5 of the SCHADS Award (MA000100), an employee who is required and authorized to use their own motor vehicle in the performance of their duties must be paid an allowance per kilometre ($1.05/km for motor vehicles and $0.35/km for motorcycles)[cite: 1].
Is travel between client appointments considered paid working time under SCHADS?
Yes. Clause 25.5 of the SCHADS Award specifies that time spent traveling between consecutive clients or locations during a shift is counted as working time and must be paid at the appropriate ordinary or overtime rate, in addition to the per-kilometre vehicle allowance[cite: 1].
Does commuting from home to the first client count for vehicle allowance?
Generally, ordinary travel from your home to your first client or workplace, and from your last client back home, is non-payable commuting travel unless specific employer direction or remote locality arrangements apply under the award[cite: 1].
What is the difference between client transport travel and travel between appointments?
Travel between appointments refers to driving your vehicle from Client A to Client B[cite: 1]. Client transport refers to driving while a client is inside your vehicle (e.g., community outings, medical visits)[cite: 1]. Both qualify for the per-km vehicle allowance ($1.05/km for cars), but client transport often involves additional insurance and safety requirements[cite: 1].
